PROTECTION OF PLANT VARIETIES AND FARMERS' RIGHTS (PPV&FR) ACT, 2001 (INDIA)
Sui Generis Plant Variety Protection, DUS Criteria, Farmers' Rights, and Benefit Sharing.

1. Legislative Framework:
India enacted the PPV&FR Act, 2001 as a unique sui generis legislation complying with Article 27.3(b) of the TRIPS Agreement while simultaneously protecting the customary rights of Indian farmers and breeders.

2. Registrable Categories of Plant Varieties (Section 14 & 15):
- New Varieties: Not in public domain or commercial exploitation in India prior to 1 year before filing.
- Extant Varieties: Notified under Seeds Act 1966 or farmers' varieties or varieties of common knowledge.
- Farmers' Varieties: Traditionally cultivated and evolved by farmers in their fields.
- Essentially Derived Varieties (EDV): Predominantly derived from an initial protected variety while retaining its essential characteristics.

3. DUS Criteria for Registration (Section 15(1)):
To be eligible for registration, a variety must satisfy four fundamental requirements:
- Distinctness (D): Clearly distinguishable by at least one essential characteristic from any other variety whose existence is a matter of common knowledge.
- Uniformity (U): Sufficiently uniform in its relevant characteristics, subject to the variation that may be expected from the particular features of its propagation.
- Stability (S): Essential characteristics remain unchanged after repeated propagation.
- Novelty (N): Propagating or harvested material has not been sold or disposed of beyond statutory time limits.

4. Farmers' Rights (Section 39):
- A farmer is entitled to save, use, sow, re-sow, exchange, share or sell his farm produce including seed of a protected variety, provided that the farmer shall not be entitled to sell branded seed of a protected variety.
- Farmers are immune from paying any registration, test, or legal fees before the Authority and are protected against innocent infringement (Section 42).

5. Benefit Sharing (Section 26):
- Tribal and rural communities contributing to genetic resources used in breeding a commercialized protected variety are entitled to statutory benefit sharing funds disbursed by the National Gene Fund.
