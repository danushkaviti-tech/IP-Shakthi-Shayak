THE PATENTS ACT, 1970 (INDIA) — SECTION 3(k)
Statutory Subject Matter: What are not inventions — Computer Programmes per se, Mathematical Methods, Business Methods, and Algorithms.

Section 3(k) of the Indian Patents Act, 1970 states that the following are NOT inventions within the meaning of this Act:
"A mathematical or business method or a computer programme per se or algorithms."

Guidelines for Examination of Computer-Related Inventions (CRIs), 2017 (Indian Patent Office):
1. Legislative Intent: The qualifier "per se" in Section 3(k) explicitly indicates that computer programmes when part of a broader technical system or when producing a concrete technical effect are not barred outright.
2. Technical Effect & Technical Contribution Test:
   - If a software-implemented invention or Artificial Intelligence (AI) model produces a tangible technical effect or solves a technical problem by improving machine performance, data communication efficiency, image processing hardware, robotic control, cryptography, or electronic system speed, the invention is patentable.
   - Claims must not be directed purely to abstract mathematical formulations, standalone source code scripts, mental concepts, or business accounting models.
   - Patent claims must be structured around hardware limitations, showing how the novel method interacts with physical processors, memory units, sensors, or external physical devices.
3. Delhi High Court Jurisprudence:
   - In Ferid Allani v. Union of India (2019), the Delhi High Court established that computer-related inventions demonstrating a technical contribution, technical advancement, or technical effect outside of a mere software program per se are patent-eligible under Section 3(k).
   - In OpenTV Inc. v. Controller of Patents (2023) and Microsoft Technology Licensing LLC (2023), the courts reiterated that digital innovations, cryptographic authentication, and system optimization techniques possessing technical utility are patentable.
4. Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning Inventions:
   - Inventions utilizing neural networks, reinforcement learning, or transformer architectures must clearly describe the specific technical dataset preprocessing, physical training architecture, and real-world system application (e.g. medical image diagnosis hardware, autonomous vehicle trajectory optimization) rather than claiming the core AI algorithm in the abstract.
