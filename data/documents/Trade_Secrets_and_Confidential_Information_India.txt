TRADE SECRETS & CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION IN INDIA
Common Law Protection, Section 27 Indian Contract Act, Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs), and Remedies.

1. Legal Framework in India:
- India does not currently possess a separate standalone statute for trade secrets. Trade secrets, proprietary algorithms, manufacturing know-how, customer lists, and financial data are vigorously protected under Common Law principles of Breach of Confidence, Equity, and the Indian Contract Act, 1872.

2. Elements to Establish a Breach of Confidence:
Under the landmark English principle (Coco v. A.N. Clark (Engineers) Ltd.) approved and consistently applied by the Supreme Court of India and High Courts:
1. Quality of Confidence: The information itself must possess the necessary quality of confidence (i.e. not public property or public knowledge).
2. Obligation of Confidence: The information must have been imparted in circumstances importing an obligation of confidence (e.g. Non-Disclosure Agreement / NDA, employment contract, fiduciary duty).
3. Unauthorized Use / Detriment: There must be unauthorized use or disclosure of that information to the detriment of the party communicating it.

3. Contractual Protection & Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872:
- Section 27 renders void every agreement by which anyone is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business.
- Post-Employment Non-Compete: Under Niranjan Shankar Golikari v. Century Spg. & Mfg. Co. (1967) and Percept D'Mark v. Zaheer Khan (2006), non-compete clauses operating strictly during the term of employment are valid and enforceable. However, post-termination non-compete restrictions are void under Section 27.
- Post-Employment Confidentiality: In sharp contrast to non-compete clauses, non-disclosure and confidentiality obligations regarding genuine trade secrets and proprietary data remain PERPETUALLY ENFORCEABLE even after termination of employment or contractual relationship (John Richard Brady v. Chemical Process Equipments P. Ltd., Delhi High Court).

4. Judicial Remedies:
- Interlocutory / Permanent Injunctions: Prohibiting the defendant or third-party recipients from disclosing, using, or commercializing the trade secret.
- Anton Piller Orders & John Doe Injunctions: Court-appointed commissioners to search and seize purloined confidential files, source code repositories, and hard drives.
- Damages or Account of Profits: Compensatory damages for business loss and unjust enrichment.
