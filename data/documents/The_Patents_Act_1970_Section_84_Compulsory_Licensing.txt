THE PATENTS ACT, 1970 (INDIA) — SECTION 84 & COMPULSORY LICENSING
Statutory Provisions, Grounds, Conditions and Landmark Precedents.

1. Application for Compulsory License (Section 84(1)):
At any time after the expiration of THREE (3) YEARS from the date of the grant of a patent, any person interested may make an application to the Controller for grant of a compulsory licence on patent on any of the following three grounds:
- Ground (a): Reasonable requirements of the public with respect to the patented invention have not been satisfied.
- Ground (b): Patented invention is not available to the public at a reasonably affordable price.
- Ground (c): Patented invention is not worked in the territory of India.

2. Settlement & Reasonable Efforts (Section 84(6)):
- The applicant must have made efforts to obtain a voluntary license from the patentee on reasonable terms and conditions, and such efforts have not succeeded within a reasonable period (normally not exceeding 6 months).

3. Landmark Precedent — Natco Pharma Ltd. v. Bayer Corporation (2012):
- First compulsory license granted in India regarding Bayer's kidney and liver cancer drug Sorafenib Tosylate (Nexavar).
- Bayer sold a monthly dosage of Nexavar at ~Rs. 2,80,000 (~$3,500), reaching less than 2% of eligible Indian patients. Natco offered the generic equivalent at ~Rs. 8,800/month (~$110).
- Controller General granted the compulsory license under Section 84, ordering Natco to pay a 6% royalty on net sales to Bayer, holding that imported supply in negligible volume does not satisfy the "working in India" and "reasonably affordable price" statutory mandates.

4. Compulsory Licenses on Notification by Central Government (Section 92):
- In circumstances of national emergency, extreme urgency, or in case of public non-commercial use (e.g. pandemic outbreak), the Central Government may issue an official notification, whereupon any person can immediately apply for a compulsory license without prior 3-year waiting or voluntary negotiation requirements.
