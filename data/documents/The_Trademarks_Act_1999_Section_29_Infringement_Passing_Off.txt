THE TRADE MARKS ACT, 1999 (INDIA) — SECTION 29 INFRINGEMENT & PASSING OFF
Statutory Infringement vs Common Law Tort of Passing Off, Remedies and Deceptive Similarity.

1. Statutory Trademark Infringement (Section 29):
A registered trade mark is infringed by a person who, not being a registered proprietor or a person using by way of permitted use, uses in the course of trade:
- Section 29(1): A mark identical with or deceptively similar to the registered mark in relation to goods or services for which the trade mark is registered.
- Section 29(2): A mark identical or similar in relation to similar goods/services creating likelihood of confusion on the part of the public.
- Section 29(4) (Dilution): A mark identical or similar to a registered mark having a reputation in India used for dissimilar goods/services where such use takes unfair advantage of or is detrimental to the distinctive character or repute of the registered mark.
- Section 29(8): Use in comparative advertising that is misleading, takes unfair advantage of, or is detrimental to distinctive character or repute of the trademark.

2. Common Law Action for Passing Off (Section 27(2)):
- Unlike infringement (which requires a registered trademark), Passing Off is a common law action based on tort protecting unregistered marks, goodwill, and trade names against deceptive misrepresentation.
- The "Classical Trinity" Test for Passing Off (Reckitt & Colman Products Ltd. v. Borden Inc., approved by Supreme Court in Cadila Healthcare v. Cadila Pharmaceuticals):
  1. Goodwill & Reputation: The plaintiff must establish goodwill or reputation attached to their goods or services.
  2. Misrepresentation: The defendant makes a representation to the public leading or likely to lead the public to believe that goods/services offered are those of the plaintiff.
  3. Damage: Likelihood of actual financial loss or damage to plaintiff's commercial goodwill.

3. Civil & Criminal Statutory Remedies:
- Civil Remedies (Section 135): Temporary / permanent injunctions, Anton Piller orders (search and seizure), damages or account of profits, and destruction of infringing goods and packaging.
- Criminal Remedies (Sections 103, 104): False application of trademark is punishable with imprisonment from 6 months up to 3 years and a fine of Rs. 50,000 up to Rs. 2,00,000.
